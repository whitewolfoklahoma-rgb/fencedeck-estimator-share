How it works

Pick Fence or Deck, enter the job, and FenceDeck counts the materials, prices them, adds labor, disposal, and your other costs, then shows three prices: Break-even = true cost + overhead. Recommended prices to your target margin. Premium = recommended + 15%. Print a clean bid sheet for the customer — it shows scope, material quantities, and the total only. Your costs and margins never print.

Enter jobs by voice

Most fields in FenceDeck Estimator have a 🎤 mic button beside them. Tap it once and speak — the words land in the field just as if you had typed them, and nothing is saved until you tap the usual save button. Numbers can be spoken naturally ("eighty feet", "posts every eight feet" for the spacing). Voice entry needs a browser/device that supports speech recognition (it works on the hosted and Google Play editions); where it isn't available, the mic shows a short note and typing works as always.

The profit floor

Your floor is a minimum margin (default 30%) on the final price. If you type a final price below the floor price, FenceDeck shows a red warning and will not save the project until you raise the price or tick the one-time override box and own the decision. The floor price also never drops below your minimum job charge. This is the guardrail that stops "busy but broke" jobs.

How fence materials are counted

Posts: one per section end — sections = fence length (minus gate openings) ÷ post spacing, rounded up, +1 post, +2 gate posts per gate. Concrete: bags per post from Settings (split rail defaults to 0 — those posts are tamped, not concreted). Wood pickets: a 5.5″ picket + ¼″ gap covers 5.75″ of fence; count = length ÷ coverage, + your waste %. Rails (wood): 2 per section at 4 ft, 3 at 6 ft, 4 at 8 ft. Chain-link: fabric in 50-ft rolls, top rail in 10-ft sections, tie/fitting packs every 25 ft. Vinyl: one panel kit (panel + rails) per section. Split rail: 3 rails per 10-ft section. Gates: one hardware & frame kit per gate. All are planning counts — walk the job and adjust prices/quantities to your supplier and style.

How deck materials are counted

Deck boards run along the length. Rows = width ÷ board coverage (5.5″ face + ¼″ gap for wood, 3/16″ for composite); boards per row = length ÷ board length, rounded up; + your waste %. Joists run across the width at your spacing (count = length ÷ spacing + 1), sized by your joist pick. Beams: beam lines = deck width ÷ the joist's planning span (2×6 ≈ 9 ft, 2×8 ≈ 12 ft, 2×10 ≈ 15 ft at 16″ o.c.), each beam doubled. Posts & footings: posts under every beam line at your max post spacing (default 6 ft); concrete bags per footing from Settings. Fasteners: a box per 100 sq ft (screws, wood) or 90 sq ft (hidden clips, composite). Railing: perimeter minus the house side (if attached) minus a 4-ft stair opening. Stairs: a per-step material allowance plus per-step labor. These are simplified planning rules, NOT engineering — check local code, span tables, and footing requirements for your soil and frost line before you build.

Localize the defaults (10 minutes)

Default material prices are national planning prices (big-box level, Oct 2026) and labor rates are mid-range planning rates. Yours will differ. Walk your last two or three jobs: set your real labor rates and concrete usage in Settings, then adjust unit prices on the Materials table per job (or leave the corrected price — it saves with the project). Sanity-check the first few estimates against actual receipts.
